## What Is a Trellis?
**trel·lis** (n.) — A framework of latticed wood or metal, fixed to the ground, that gives a climbing plant something to grow against. The plant supplies the growth; the trellis supplies the structure — without it, the same plant sprawls along the ground instead of climbing.

A website's content is the plant. What it's missing isn't ambition, it's structure — a prioritized order to work in, a place to track what's done, a way to see progress. That's what Trellis supplies; the site owner still writes the content, fixes the pages, publishes the posts. It's also a quiet, deliberate pun: unpaid search visibility has been called "organic" search since before AEO existed, and a trellis is literally garden infrastructure.

## Brand Positioning
Tools priced for a solo marketer stop at insight (a score, a report). Tools that carry work through to done are priced for a team with budget. Trellis is full execution at a zero-budget entry point — not "SEO + AEO in one place" (table stakes per the competitive landscape), but the only one of these that stays with a recommendation from Suggested to Published, free.

## Mission
**To give every under-resourced marketer a complete SEO & AEO strategy — audited, prioritized, and carried through to published — without ever asking them for a budget they don't have.**

Positioning says where Trellis sits in the market; mission says why it gets out of bed — the sentence every roadmap decision, dismissed feature idea, and pricing conversation has to answer to. Three deliberate commitments inside it:
- **"Complete strategy," not a partial one** — depth across technical, local, content, and AEO, not just breadth of buzzwords. The moment Trellis stops at a score and calls it done, it's become the tool it was built to replace.
- **"Carried through to published," not just suggested** — a recommendation nobody acts on didn't help anyone. Feature decisions get weighed against whether they move a suggestion closer to done.
- **"Without asking for a budget they don't have," not "at a discount"** — a promise that the free tier is a real strategy tool, not a demo designed to expire into a paywall. If Trellis ever monetizes (the spec floats $15–40/month for a future tier), that line has to hold for the marketer who genuinely can't pay.

## Values & Purpose
1. **Access, not exclusivity** — a real audit-to-plan workflow at the free tier, no sales call, no $79+/month floor.
2. **Clarity, not jargon** — every recommendation carries a one-sentence plain-language reason it matters for this specific site.
3. **Momentum, not perfection** — the Health Score exists to show movement since last time, not to judge (61 → 68 is the story).
4. **Follow-through, not a report** — every finding is carried visibly from Suggested through to Published.

## Target Audience
- **Primary — Priya, the one-person marketing department.** Marketing manager at a 12-person nonprofit, or a solo marketer serving several small-business clients. Owns marketing end to end, no specialist, no budget. Feels quietly behind on AEO/AI visibility; uses spreadsheets today. Needs to feel capable, not lectured. Low-to-moderate technical comfort — needs the "why" before the "how."
- **Secondary — the agency account manager.** Runs the same audit-and-plan motion across multiple client sites; values consistency and speed over deep customization.

## Brand Story
Every website needs something to grow, and structure to grow along — a garden fails not for lack of ambition but for lack of support at the moment it needed to climb. Full-service consultancies know how to build that structure but charge thousands/month. "Affordable" AEO tools ($79–199/mo) stop at a score or report and leave the actual climbing — deciding what to write, tracking it, publishing it — back in a spreadsheet. Trellis starts from the free tier and builds up to consultancy-grade depth (technical audits, local SEO, AI-citation checks, competitor visibility), instead of starting from consultancy pricing and working down. Mission: give a strategy to the businesses who could never afford a specialist, not replace one for the businesses who already can.

## Voice & Personality
Talks like a capable friend who happens to know SEO — never a vendor, never a professor. Warm, direct, quietly confident; never makes the user feel behind.

| Trellis says | Not this |
|---|---|
| "Here's what I'd fix first, and why." | "The following issues were detected during the crawl." |
| "Your Health Score moved from 61 to 68 this week." | "Your SEO performance metrics have been recalculated." |
| "3 new keyword opportunities since your last scan." | "Please review the updated keyword corpus." |
| "Not relevant right now? Tell us why and we'll skip it." | "Dismiss recommendation." |

- **Type:** Fraunces (display — headlines, tagline, and the logo wordmark itself), Public Sans (body — UI copy, long-form text), IBM Plex Mono (utility — labels, metadata, numbers like "68 ▲ +7"). Final logo files live in `Branding/New final logo/`.

## How It Shows Up in Product
The Health Score + since-last-scan delta (already spec'd as FR-1.2 / FR-11) is where the brand's "climbing" idea and "ally" voice meet. Lead the Website Overview with this number, large, before keywords or pipeline counts — the delta is the proof of momentum that brings the user back.
