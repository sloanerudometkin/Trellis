# Trellis Imagery, Iconography, and Motion

## Creative principle

Show **structured growth**, not generic gardening and not futuristic AI. Visuals should make complicated work feel organized, achievable, and calm.

## Product imagery

- Prefer real interface compositions over decorative mockups.
- Demonstrate one story per image: a Health Score improving, a recommendation entering the plan, or work reaching Published.
- Use realistic example data and clearly label illustrative data when necessary.
- Do not show features, integrations, rankings, or results the product cannot provide.
- Keep product screenshots readable; never shrink an entire dashboard into decoration.

## Illustration

- Use restrained editorial diagrams built from open grids, climbing paths, structured rows, and measured progress.
- Use Paper and Sage Tint as dominant fields, Ink and Moss Deep for structure, Moss for progress, and Gold once for emphasis.
- Avoid stock leaves, dense botanical borders, photorealistic gardens, robots, glowing brains, neon gradients, and circuit-board imagery.
- The trellis metaphor appears at major story moments only. Routine controls remain literal and familiar.

## Photography

- If photography is used, show capable people doing real work in modest, believable environments: a nonprofit office, small team, or independent workspace.
- Favor natural light, quiet confidence, and documentary framing.
- Avoid staged handshakes, luxury offices, exaggerated frustration, and images that portray the audience as helpless.

## Interface icons

- Use one consistent rounded, simple line-icon family.
- Default size: 20px in controls and 24px in navigation or feature summaries.
- Default stroke: approximately 1.75–2px with rounded caps and joins.
- Icons support text; they do not replace unfamiliar labels.
- Do not reuse the Trellis logo’s vine or leaves as routine interface icons.

## Diagrams

- The official six-step story is Analyze → Recommend → Prioritize → Complete → Rescan → Measure progress.
- Use a horizontal sequence on wide screens and a vertical sequence on narrow screens.
- Give every step a verb, a one-sentence outcome, and a visible connection to the next step.

## Motion

- Motion explains progression, change, or relationship.
- Use 150–250ms for interface transitions and up to 500ms for a meaningful progress reveal.
- Avoid looping decorative growth animations, parallax, bouncing calls to action, and motion that delays reading.
- Provide an equivalent static state and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
