# Trellis — Brand Brief (v3.0, September 2026)

**Recommended name:** Trellis
**Tagline:** "Give your content something to climb."

## What Is a Trellis?
**trel·lis** (n.) — A framework of latticed wood or metal, fixed to the ground, that gives a climbing plant something to grow against. The plant supplies the growth; the trellis supplies the structure — without it, the same plant sprawls along the ground instead of climbing.

A website's content is the plant. What it's missing isn't ambition, it's structure — a prioritized order to work in, a place to track what's done, a way to see progress. That's what Trellis supplies; the site owner still writes the content, fixes the pages, publishes the posts, and decides what (if anything) to spend on ads. It's also a quiet, deliberate pun: unpaid search visibility has been called "organic" search since before AEO existed, and a trellis is literally garden infrastructure.

## Brand Positioning
Tools priced for a solo marketer often stop at insight: a score or report. Tools that carry work through to done are usually designed and priced for larger teams. Trellis is designed around access and follow-through—not merely “SEO + AEO in one place,” but a workspace that stays with a recommendation from Suggested to Published and pairs cost-conscious paid-search planning with the organic strategy instead of leaving it in another tool or spreadsheet.

## Mission
**To make a complete organic-and-paid search growth strategy accessible to under-resourced marketers—audited, prioritized, and carried through to published.**

Positioning says where Trellis sits in the market; mission says why it gets out of bed — the sentence every roadmap decision, dismissed feature idea, and pricing conversation has to answer to. Three deliberate commitments inside it:
- **"Complete strategy," not a partial one** — depth across technical, local, content, AEO, and paid search, not just breadth of buzzwords. The moment Trellis stops at a score and calls it done, it's become the tool it was built to replace.
- **"Carried through to published," not just suggested** — a recommendation nobody acts on didn't help anyone. Feature decisions get weighed against whether they move a suggestion closer to done.
- **“Accessible to under-resourced marketers,” not “cheap”** — pricing, limits, and future monetization must be communicated plainly and validated before launch. Affordability is a product principle; an unapproved permanent-free promise is not.

## Values & Purpose
1. **Access, not exclusivity** — reduce financial, technical, and language barriers; publish pricing and limits plainly once the customer offer is finalized.
2. **Clarity, not jargon** — every recommendation carries a one-sentence plain-language reason it matters for this specific site.
3. **Momentum, not perfection** — the Health Score exists to show movement since last time, not to judge (61 → 68 is the story).
4. **Follow-through, not a report** — every finding is carried visibly from Suggested through to Published.

## Target Audience
- **Primary — Priya, the one-person marketing department.** Marketing manager at a 12-person nonprofit, or a solo marketer serving several small-business clients. Owns marketing end to end — organic and paid alike — with no dedicated SEO/AEO/SEM specialist and no budget. Feels quietly behind on AEO/AI visibility, and unsure whether or where paid search would be worth the spend; uses spreadsheets today. Needs to feel capable, not lectured. Low-to-moderate technical comfort — needs the "why" before the "how."
- **Secondary — the agency account manager.** Runs the same audit-and-plan motion across multiple client sites; values consistency and speed over deep customization.

## Brand Story
Every website needs something to grow and structure to grow along—a garden fails not for lack of ambition, but for lack of support at the moment it needed to climb. Full-service consultancies can build that structure but are often out of reach. Many self-serve tools stop at a score or report and leave the actual climbing—deciding what to write, tracking it, publishing it, and understanding whether paid search is worth trying—back in a spreadsheet. Trellis brings technical audits, content and AEO guidance, paid-search planning, organized execution, and progress reporting into one approachable workspace. Its purpose is to make useful strategy available to teams that have historically gone without it.

## Website Story & Message Hierarchy
The marketing website leads with the customer's outcome, not unexplained category terminology. SEO, AEO, and SEM support the promise; they are not the first thing a visitor must decode.

1. **Outcome:** Know what to fix, what to create, and what to do next.
2. **Problem:** Most affordable tools find issues and leave the user with another report or spreadsheet.
3. **Difference:** Trellis carries recommendations through one visible workflow: **Analyze → Recommend → Prioritize → Complete → Measure progress.**
4. **Proof:** Show a realistic Health Score change, prioritized recommendation, task status, and next action.
5. **Scope:** Explain SEO, AEO, and SEM in plain language as three connected parts of one search strategy.
6. **Audience:** State explicitly that Trellis is built for solo marketers, nonprofits, and small teams.
7. **Access:** Explain exactly what the current offer includes, its limits, and whether payment is required. Never imply permanent free access unless product pricing has formally committed to it.
8. **Action:** Invite the visitor to add a site and build a first plan.

Recommended homepage hero:

> **Give your content something to climb.**
>
> Trellis turns your website audit into a prioritized SEO, AEO, and paid-search plan—and helps you carry every recommendation through to published.

- Primary action: **Build my first plan**
- Secondary action: **See how Trellis works**

Use the garden metaphor selectively. Progress may "climb," Trellis supplies structure, and the user's content supplies growth. Do not give every feature a plant-themed name or allow decorative garden language to obscure the task.

## Voice & Personality
Talks like a capable friend who happens to know SEO — never a vendor, never a professor. Warm, direct, quietly confident; never makes the user feel behind.

| Trellis says | Not this |
|---|---|
| "Here's what I'd fix first, and why." | "The following issues were detected during the crawl." |
| "Your Health Score moved from 61 to 68 this week." | "Your SEO performance metrics have been recalculated." |
| "3 new keyword opportunities since your last scan." | "Please review the updated keyword corpus." |
| "Not relevant right now? Tell us why and we'll skip it." | "Dismiss recommendation." |

## How It Shows Up in Product
The Health Score + since-last-scan delta (already spec'd as FR-1.2 / FR-7) is where the brand's "climbing" idea and "ally" voice meet. Lead the Website Overview with this number, large, before keywords or pipeline counts — the delta is the proof of momentum that brings the user back. The SEM section carries its own small summary alongside it (accepted keyword count, estimated cost range) rather than folding into the Health Score, since paid and organic are different disciplines with different success signals (spec FR-7.3, FR-8.8).

Every important screen should answer three questions in this order: **What happened? Why does it matter? What should I do next?** The primary next action must be visually obvious. Recommendations should show their place in the audit-to-published workflow rather than behaving like a static report.

## Experience Principles
1. **Recommendation before methodology** — show the next useful action before technical detail.
2. **One clear next step** — avoid competing primary buttons and dashboard overload.
3. **Progress without judgment** — emphasize movement since the previous scan, not a frightening score in isolation.
4. **Plain-language learning** — explain necessary SEO/AEO/SEM terms close to where they appear.
5. **Accessible by default** — readable contrast, keyboard access, visible focus, descriptive labels, and no meaning communicated by color alone.
6. **Prove before polishing** — test desktop and mobile prototypes with at least five target users before treating the website design as final.

## Offer Language Before Pricing Is Finalized
- The current product specification guarantees a $0 build-and-demo stack; it does not guarantee permanent customer pricing.
- Approved pre-launch language: **accessible**, **cost-conscious**, **built for under-resourced marketers**, and **no consultant required**.
- Conditional language such as **free**, **free tier**, **no credit card**, or a dollar amount may be published only after the launch offer and limits are formally defined.
- When pricing is finalized, state what is included, usage limits, renewal behavior, and upgrade triggers beside the primary call to action.
